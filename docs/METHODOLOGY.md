# Atlas Methodology

Atlas is a maintained research database for purchasing decisions. The unit of analysis is the **product**. A product may have multiple listings, sellers, batches, and evidence records.

## Research loop

1. **Observe** — discover a candidate without drawing conclusions.
2. **Collect** — gather primary and secondary evidence.
3. **Compare** — identify alternatives, duplicates, contradictions, and better-known batches.
4. **Conclude** — assign a decision only after evidence review.
5. **Revisit** — update the record when new evidence appears.

## Evidence hierarchy

### Tier S — Direct evidence
Exact-listing warehouse QC; contributor-owned in-hand photos/measurements; marketplace listing data; exact-item measurements; confirmed weight.

### Tier A — Strong corroboration
Multiple independent in-hand reviews; batch comparisons; recent item-specific Reddit QC; JadeShip purchase/QC history.

### Tier B — Discovery/context
GlobalFind; community spreadsheets; Yupoo catalogs; seller storefronts; aggregators.

### Tier C — Weak evidence
Seller marketing; promotional content; recycled stock photos; uncorroborated “1:1” or “best batch” claims.

## Product acceptance

Approval requires exact identity, active/documented listing, exact-listing QC when relevant, seller/current price, recent activity, duplicate/alternative review, available physical data, contradictory evidence, and a reproducible rationale.

## Facts vs interpretations

Facts such as measured weight, chest width, and price remain separate from interpretations such as heavyweight, oversized, or good value.

## Community evidence

Atlas stores structured signals instead of a single community star rating: positive/negative in-hand reports, complaint themes, praise themes, disagreement, recency, and source independence.

## Images

A frontal QC image must be traceable to the exact listing or confirmed exact batch. Retail photos, seller stock photos, similar items, and AI-generated images may not be labeled as warehouse QC.

## Negative knowledge

Rejected and problematic listings are preserved to prevent repeated research and repeated mistakes.
